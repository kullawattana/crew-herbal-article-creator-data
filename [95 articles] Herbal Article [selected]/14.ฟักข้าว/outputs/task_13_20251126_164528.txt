> **Scientific Research:** 
> beta-Carotene, a potent natural antioxidant, was recovered from the peel of Momordica cochinchinensis (gac) fruit peel using Soxhlet extraction. Experimental results indicated that both the sample mass and solvent flow rate exerted a positive influence on the yield of beta-carotene. The beta-carotene extraction achieved the highest yield with a solvent mixture of ethyl acetate and acetone in a 6 : 4 (v/v) ratio. Thermal stability assessments revealed that beta-carotene exhibited greater stability in acetone compared to ethyl acetate under oxygenated conditions. A kinetic model was developed to describe the beta-carotene extraction, facilitating the estimation of key parameters including the extraction rate constant, degradation rate constant, and the maximum extractable beta-carotene in the gac peel. The model demonstrated good correlation with experimental data, providing valuable insights for optimizing beta-carotene extraction protocols.

## Traditional Wisdom
Momordica cochinchinensis has cultural significance in various communities. For instance, in Wat Phra Mahathat Woramahawihan, located in Nakhon Si Thammarat Province, it is known as "Fakkhao" and used as an offering. In Ban Sop Lan, Chiang Mai Province, it is also referred to as "Fakkhao" and used for planting. Similarly, in Pha To, Mae Hong Son Province, it is used for planting. These traditional uses highlight the importance of Momordica cochinchinensis in local cultures and its potential for development into culturally inspired wellness products.
