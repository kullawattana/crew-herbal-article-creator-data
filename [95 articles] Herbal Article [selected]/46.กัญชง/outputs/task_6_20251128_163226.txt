# ===TOXICITY_DATA=== ## Clinical Toxicity Data for: Cannabis sativa * **herb_name:** Cannabis sativa * **source_browsed:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK556048/ ### Clinical Summary * **toxicity_summary:** Cannabidiol (CBD) is a nonintoxicating compound found in the plant Cannabis sativa. It has been shown to have several beneficial pharmacological effects, including analgesic and anti-inflammatory activities. However, it can also cause dose-dependent hepatotoxicity, and its use has been associated with liver damage. Concomitant use of CBD and other medications, including leflunomide, lomitapide, mipomersen, pexidartinib, teriflunomide, and valproate, is known to cause liver damage. * **mechanism_of_injury:** The mechanism of action for cannabidiol, especially concerning its anticonvulsant effect, has not been fully elucidated. CBD is known to have a low affinity for cannabinoid receptors CB1 and CB2, where it can act as an agonist or antagonist. The anti-inflammatory effects of CBD may be explained by its inverse agonist activity at the CB2 receptor. * **reported_side_effects:** Reports of malaise, asthenia, and sedation associated with CBD use have been documented. These adverse effects may diminish over time and are more likely to be reported earlier in treatment. A meta-analysis demonstrated that CBD treatment is linked to somnolence, diarrhea, and transaminitis. CBD is associated with worsening respiratory depression in patients taking other respiratory-depressing drugs, such as opioids and benzodiazepines. * **case_report_summary:** There have been reports of required ventilation support for children and older adults after ingesting excessive cannabis amounts. CBD is only recently available and is usually used as an adjunct therapy; further investigations are necessary to better comprehend potential adverse effects and effects on liver enzymes and drug interactions.
