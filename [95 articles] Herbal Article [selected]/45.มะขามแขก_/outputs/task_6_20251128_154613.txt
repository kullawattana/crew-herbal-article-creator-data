# ===TOXICITY_DATA=== ## Clinical Toxicity Data for: Senna alexandrina * **herb_name:** Senna alexandrina * **source_browsed:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576229/ ### Clinical Summary * **toxicity_summary:** Senna alexandrina, also known as Cassia acutifolia or Cassia angustifolia, is a plant used as a laxative and stimulant. The active components in senna extracts are anthraquinone derivatives and their glucosides, referred to as senna glycosides or sennosides. Senna is generally safe and well tolerated, but can cause adverse events including clinically apparent liver injury when used in high doses for longer than recommended periods. * **mechanism_of_injury:** The liver injury due to senna has been attributed to the anthraquinone derivatives present in the herbal extract, and most cases have been associated with taking excessive doses suggesting a direct hepatotoxic rather than idiosyncratic etiology. * **reported_side_effects:** Abdominal cramps, diarrhea, electrolyte imbalance, liver injury, and skin irritation. * **case_report_summary:** There have been several case reports of liver injury associated with the use of senna, including cases of acute liver failure and hepatomyoencephalopathy. In some cases, reexposure to senna has led to rapid recurrence of liver injury.
