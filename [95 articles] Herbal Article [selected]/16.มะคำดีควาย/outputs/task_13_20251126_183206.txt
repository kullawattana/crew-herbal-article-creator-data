# Sapindus Rarak: Unlocking the Potential of Soap Nut in Wellness and Beyond
## Herbal in Wellness Trends
Sapindus rarak, commonly known as Soap Nut or Reetha, has been utilized for centuries in traditional medicine, particularly in Ayurvedic practices, for its natural and effective properties as a surfactant. It is hypoallergenic and safe for sensitive skin, making it an ideal ingredient for personal care and cosmetic products. The benefits of Soap Nut include promoting hair growth, improving hair thickness, and preventing hair fall, as well as treating various health issues such as acne, dandruff, and hair breakage. With the growing demand for natural and organic products, Sapindus rarak presents a significant market opportunity for new product development in the wellness industry.

> **Scientific Research:**
> High-purity CaO cubic crystallites extracted from limestone exhibited excellent activity as base catalysts for waste cooking oil (WCO) conversion into biodiesel. Saponin from Sapindus rarak extract acted as a surfactant in CaO extraction and transformation into well-defined cubic microcrystallites. The application of saponin from Sapindus rarak extract as a surfactant for CaO production results in a high level of CaO purity and particle size reduction compared to directly calcined limestone (CaO-MgO). The catalytic activity was evaluated on CaO from hydrothermal and co-precipitation synthesis, MgO and CaO-MgO derived from limestone, giving hydrothermal CaO catalysts enhanced biodiesel yield. Optimization of transesterification conditions using Box Behnken Design response surface methodology achieved 92.40% biodiesel yield at 65 degrees C, 3 h reaction time and when using 5% of CaO catalysts.
> Saponin is a plant-derived chemical with an amphiphilic glycoconjugate structure extracted from sapindaceae plants like Sapindus rarak. This study investigated saponin extract of Sapindus rarak as a natural template for formation of mesoporous zeolite Y. Surface area and mesoporosity of zeolite Y were improved with optimization of Sapindus rarak extract (SRE) concentration (Y-Ln; n = 2, 5, 10 or 15 mL), reaching 216.26 m(2) mesoporous area and 0.214 cm(3) g(-1) mesoporous volume for Y-L10 samples. A different loading of Ni was impregnated onto Y-L10 zeolite to improve Lewis/Bronsted acidity as catalysts in the deoxygenation of Reutealis trisperma oil (RTO) into hydrocarbon fuels. Impregnating 15% Ni on NaY zeolite enhanced Lewis acidity to 0.4556 mmol g(-1), producing 48.8% liquid oil with 85.43% degree of deoxygenation. A high selectivity towards C15 and C17 hydrocarbon was analyzed from liquid yield, indicating the contributing factor from Lewis acidity and mesoporosity to enhance deoxygenation and prevent the hydrocracking reaction.

## Traditional Wisdom
The traditional use of Sapindus rarak is deeply rooted in the cultural practices of various communities. Among the Shan people in Chiang Mai Province, Sapindus rarak is known as "Makham Dee Kwai" and is used for laundry. In Ban Kang Pla, Loei Province, it is also referred to as "Makham Dee Kwai" but is utilized for treating skin conditions. The use of Sapindus rarak in these communities highlights its versatility and the importance of traditional knowledge in harnessing the benefits of natural resources.
