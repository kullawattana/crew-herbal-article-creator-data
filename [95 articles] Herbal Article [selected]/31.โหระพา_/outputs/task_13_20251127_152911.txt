## Traditional Wisdom
Sweet basil has been deeply rooted in the traditional wisdom of various communities, including Ban Muang Noi, Jungfood Cafe, and Ban Klang. In these communities, basil is not only used in cooking but also valued for its medicinal properties. The herb is believed to have healing properties and is used to treat various ailments, such as fever and digestive issues. Additionally, basil is used as a natural remedy for skin problems and is believed to have antioxidant properties. The traditional farming methods and local wisdom surrounding the use of basil highlight its significance in these communities.

## Safety, Regulatory, and Constraints
The safety and regulatory status of Ocimum basilicum is complex. While basil is considered safe in amounts found in food, its use in medicinal doses, especially during lactation, poses risks due to the lack of safety information and the potential procarcinogenic effects of estragole. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration considers basil as "generally recognized as safe" (GRAS) as a food, but the absence of scientifically valid clinical trials supporting its medicinal use, particularly as a galactogogue, necessitates caution. Any product development must carefully address these safety concerns.
