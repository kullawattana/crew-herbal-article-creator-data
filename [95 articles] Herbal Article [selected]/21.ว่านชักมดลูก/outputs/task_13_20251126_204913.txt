## Scientific Deep-Dive
Curcuma comosa, with its active compound diarylheptanoid, has been studied for its antibacterial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties. The combination of Curcuma comosa with Garcinia mangostana and Acanthus ebracteatus has been shown to effectively treat skin breakouts and promote overall skin health. 
> **Scientific Research:** 
> Garcinia mangostana (GM), Curcuma comosa (CC), and Acanthus ebracteatus (AE) are traditionally used to treat different illnesses because of their antibacterial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties. We utilized a mix of in silico, in vitro, and molecular dynamics (MD) techniques to assess their abilities to treat skin breakouts. We found that the active ingredients in these herbs significantly interact with several bacterial and human inflammatory proteins. Specifically, the herbal components strongly bind to the bacterial enzyme topoisomerase II alpha (TOP2α), which is fundamental for bacterial DNA replication and repair, as well as to human nuclear factor kappa B subunit 1 (NF-κB1), which is a vital marker of inflammation. The findings from this study suggest that this combination treatment offers two advantages, preventing bacterial growth and reducing inflammation, making it a promising choice for skin breakouts. In addition, this combination was not toxic to fibroblasts, supporting its capacity as a safe skin treatment. Interestingly, at a 15:5:10 GM:CC:AE ratio, this combination treatment displayed high selectivity indices (SIs) against Staphylococcus epidermidis (26.87) and C. acnes (4.99), indicating its strong therapeutic potential without side effects. In conclusion, our study highlights that this combination is a novel, effective, and safe choice for controlling acne vulgaris, especially because of its ability to inhibit TOP2α and regulate NF-κB1.
The optimal ratios of these herbs, as determined by the studies, provide a basis for developing effective and safe products.

## Traditional Wisdom
The community of Ban Namjoung is known for its herbal wisdom, particularly in the use of ginger and other herbs for medicinal purposes. The community is located in Phitsanulok and is part of the Hmong ethnic group. The villagers have adapted to the mountainous environment by growing crops suitable for high altitude and have a strong tradition of using herbal medicine. Curcuma comosa, locally known as Wan Chak Mod Look, is used for herbal medicine and is processed through drying. Similarly, the community of Bang Phli Old Market in Samut Prakan, with a Thai-Chinese ethnic background, is known for its traditional herbal shops selling various herbs and medicinal plants, including Curcuma comosa, which is used for herbal medicine and packaged for sale.
