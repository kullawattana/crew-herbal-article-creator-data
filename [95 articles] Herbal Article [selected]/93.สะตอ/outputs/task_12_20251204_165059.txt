# ===STRATEGIC_PLAN=== 
## Strategic Plan for New Product Development Based on Master Fact Sheet
* **Market Opportunity:** The Trend Facts suggest a market opportunity for Parkia speciosa, also known as Bitter Bean or Sato, in the Asian traditions of wellness, where it is used as a food ingredient. The plant has been used in traditional Malaysian cooking and has various health benefits, including aiding digestion and relieving symptoms of certain diseases. The growing interest in wellness policies that help older populations stay active and the importance of proper sanitation practices in geothermal hot springs also present opportunities for Parkia speciosa-based products. The fact that Parkia speciosa has been registered as a food product in various companies, with registration numbers and product names available, indicates a potential market for Parkia speciosa-based food products.
* **Technical Feasibility:** The Lab Facts indicate that Parkia speciosa contains the compound 1,2,4-Trithiolane, which has been identified as having antioxidant and cardioprotective activities. The study on the cardioprotective potential of hydrogen sulfide (H2S) released by oil extracted from Parkia speciosa Hassk. seeds (PsH) also suggests that Parkia speciosa has potential health benefits. However, the lack of information on extraction methods, chromatography, and quality control may pose technical challenges. The studies on the effect of boiling, steaming, and microwaving on the antioxidant and antibacterial properties of Parkia speciosa seeds provide valuable information on the processing of Parkia speciosa.
* **Critical Constraints (Safety/Toxicity):** The Toxicity Facts indicate that Parkia speciosa has been found to have antioxidant and cardioprotective activities, as well as antibacterial properties against human pathogenic bacteria. However, the toxicity study is also lacking, and no in vivo toxicity study has been carried out. The cause of liver injury due to Parkia speciosa is unknown, and case reports of liver injury in patients taking Parkia speciosa have been reported, with symptoms including fever, fatigue, and jaundice. The side effects of Parkia speciosa include nausea, dermatitis, rash, diarrhea, dysentery, hypertension, cough, chronic piles, conjunctivitis, and measles. These safety and toxicity concerns must be addressed in the development of Parkia speciosa-based products.
* **Strategic Recommendation:** Based on the Master Fact Sheet, a strategic recommendation for new product development is to focus on creating Parkia speciosa-based food products that leverage its antioxidant and cardioprotective activities, as well as its antibacterial properties. The products should be designed to address the safety and toxicity concerns associated with Parkia speciosa, such as liver injury and side effects. The development of Parkia speciosa-based products should also take into account the technical challenges posed by the lack of information on extraction methods, chromatography, and quality control. Additionally, the products should be marketed to the Asian traditions of wellness, where Parkia speciosa is already used as a food ingredient, and to the growing interest in wellness policies that help older populations stay active. The registration of Parkia speciosa as a food product in various companies should also be leveraged to ensure compliance with regulatory requirements.
